# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Developers who monitor and direct Codex work from iPhone, iPad, Android, or
Mac Catalyst while their agents and terminals run locally or on paired and
SSH-connected computers. They may move between several coding-agent harnesses,
servers, repositories, and ambient-light conditions during one session.

## Product Purpose

Remora is the native mobile control surface for local and remote coding-agent
work. It keeps sessions, approvals, voice, discovery, pairing, and remote
terminals coherent across platforms so developers can move away from their
desk without losing context or control. Success means pairing is dependable,
multi-host work remains legible, and platform-specific features feel native
without duplicating shared runtime policy.

## Brand Personality

Capable, fluid, and quietly distinctive. Remora should feel technically
serious and responsive, with an aquatic identity drawn from its mascot rather
than novelty copy or decorative effects.

## Anti-references

- The original fork's black-and-neon-green terminal identity.
- Generic hacker-terminal styling that uses green for every action and state.
- Stock blue SaaS or AI dashboards with gradients, glass cards, and ornamental
  color that does not communicate state.
- Platform skins that override or recolor user-selected terminal themes.

## Design Principles

1. Keep the developer in flow: navigation, pairing, approvals, and recovery
   should be immediate and predictable.
2. Use one restrained Remora identity across platforms while respecting native
   interaction conventions.
3. Treat pairing and multi-harness interoperability as primary product
   strengths, not setup details.
4. Keep shared behavior in the Rust runtime and platform presentation thin so
   parity is the default.
5. Let content and state lead; brand color supports hierarchy but never becomes
   the only way information is communicated.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA contrast for text and controls, preserve Dynamic Type and
system font scaling, support reduced-motion preferences, and pair semantic
colors with labels, shapes, or icons. Validate the default palette in light and
dark modes and avoid red/green-only distinctions.
